2. B had the strongest RF design, with the radome datum and the gap in one part. Resubmitting B with a listed, thickness-stated clear rod grade (or with no separate pipe) would probably make it A's main rival.
3. A's advantages over the datum come from its architecture: kit and cable on the fixed part, tool-free twist-off, a uniform radome and a groove in every build. Its main technical weakness is that the antenna gap crosses the bayonet joint.
4. D's retention also looks marginal under S1: a helical lead with 45 deg conical flanks and only a bump detent. I passed it, but a reviewer should check it if D is revised.
5. None of the concepts moves the US$30 BOM, since the kit alone is US$28.99. All four depend on 60 GHz coupon permittivity measurements (TP-SYS-002) and on a colour or coating route the UL listings do not cover.
