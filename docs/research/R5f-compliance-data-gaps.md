## Summary
- **UK is a separate market with its own statutes.** The Radio Equipment Regulations 2017 (SI 2017/1206, in force 26 Dec 2017) set the essential requirements in regulation 6: health and safety (6(1)(a)), EMC (6(1)(b)) and efficient use of spectrum (6(2)). [V]
- **The UK EMC Regulations 2016 (SI 2016/1091, in force 21 Dec 2016) do not apply to equipment covered by the Radio Equipment Regulations 2017** (regulation 3(2)(a), GB version). So for radio equipment, UK EMC sits under RER 2017 regulation 6(1)(b), and the EMC Regulations are a candidate only for products without a radio. [V]
- **PSTI:** Part 1 of the Product Security and Telecommunications Infrastructure Act 2022 (c. 46, Royal Assent 6 Dec 2022) regulates "relevant connectable products" (internet- or network-connectable, section 4). The security requirements are in SI 2023/1007, in force **29 Apr 2024**, Schedule 1: passwords; information on how to report security issues; information on minimum security update periods. [V]
- **UKCA route:** gov.uk "Using the UKCA marking" (last updated 21 Aug 2026): UKCA applies in Great Britain; CE marking stays accepted in GB for most regimes including RER 2017 and EMC Regs 2016, under the Product Safety and Metrology (Amendment) Regulations 2024; Northern Ireland follows separate rules. [V]
- **UK designated standards:** the GB consolidated list for RER 2017 (version 10, 21 Jan 2026) designates **EN 300 328 V2.2.2**, **EN 301 489-17 V3.3.1** (from 19 Jul 2025, with two restrictions), **EN 301 489-3 V2.3.2** (from 19 Jul 2025) and **EN 305 550-2 V1.2.1** (from 1 Jan 2021, with a receiver-parameter restriction). EN 301 489-1 is not a separate row in v10; it is cited as a normative reference inside the part-specific rows. [V]
- **EU RED core essential requirements** (Directive 2014/53/EU, adopted 16 Apr 2014), Article 3: 3(1)(a) health and safety, with the Low Voltage Directive's safety objectives but no voltage limit; 3(1)(b) an adequate level of EMC as in Directive 2014/30/EU; 3(2) effective use of spectrum to avoid harmful interference. [V, via legislation.gov.uk]
- **ETSI editions (read on ETSI's own deliver server):** EN 300 328 **V2.2.2 (2019-07)** (2.4 GHz wideband data, scope names RED art. 3.2); EN 301 489-1 **V2.2.3 (2019-11)** (common EMC, art. 3.1(b)); EN 301 489-17 **V3.3.1 (2024-09)** (broadband/wideband data, scope table includes EN 300 328 equipment and 60 GHz MGWS/WDTS but **not** 60 GHz radar); EN 301 489-3 **V2.3.2 (2023-01)** (SRD 9 kHz to 246 GHz, which is the EMC part for a 60 GHz radar SRD). [V]
- **60 GHz consumer radar in the EU has no published ETSI part yet.** The EN 305 550 series was re-planned as six parts (EN 305 550-6 V1.2.1 foreword); **Part 3, "Radiodetermination for consumer applications within 57 GHz to 64 GHz, ..."** is the one for a presence radar, but ETSI's deliver folder list on 2026-09-25 shows only 305550, 30555001, 30555002, 30555005 and 30555006: **no published EN 305 550-3**. The older EN 305 550-2 V1.2.1 (2014, R&TTE-era, 40 to 246 GHz) is UK-designated [V]; its EU status under RED is [U]. A qualified human decides the EU conformity-assessment route.
- **FCC (eCFR, issue date 2026-09-23):** section 15.247 (902-928 MHz, 2400-2483.5 MHz, 5725-5850 MHz; frequency hopping and digitally modulated intentional radiators), last amended 85 FR 18149 (1 Apr 2020); section 15.255 (57-71 GHz, including field disturbance sensors/radars with their own EIRP and conducted-power limits in 15.255(c)(2)-(3)), last amended 88 FR 47394 (24 Jul 2023). [V]
- **ISED:** RSS-247 Issue 4 (24 Jul 2025) already in R5e [V]. **RSS-210 Issue 11 (25 Jun 2024)**, Annex J covers 57-71 GHz devices including field disturbance sensors and radars; six-month transition from publication. [V]
- **IEC 62368-1 scope has no power-source condition.** The IEC abstract (Ed. 3.0 page) says it applies to electrical and electronic equipment in audio, video, information and communication technology, and business and office machines, with a rated voltage not exceeding 600 V. Current edition: Ed. 4.0, 2023-05-26 (IEC webstore page 69308). [V for Ed. 3.0 scope sentence and Ed. 4.0 title/edition/date; R for the Ed. 4.0 scope sentence]. So a USB- or adapter-powered ICT sensor is a candidate; the old `power_source in mains` trigger was narrower than both the standard's scope and R5b's own trigger text ("mains- or battery-powered ... sensors with a mains adapter").
- **Market codes:** ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 assigns US (United States) and CA (Canada); GB is the assigned code for the United Kingdom; **EU and UK are "exceptionally reserved"** codes (EU at the EU's request; UK at the UK's request). Forge uses **US, CA, EU, UK**, and accepts GB as an alias of UK. [R]
